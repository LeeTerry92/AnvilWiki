**Source snapshot:** Public WARDOGS Field Guide, reviewed September 14, 2026. Official announcements and historical community test-build data are distinct; prices, availability and planned features may change.

## Season 1 spending and post-launch economy rules

The launch changelog raises the vendor FOB price to $7,500 and the vendor Large Hammer to $2,400. Unlock-track costs and vendor purchase costs are different entries; do not mix them in a loadout budget.

Patch 0.11 announces a temporary community-server bonus of 5% end-of-match cash for one week. It is not an all-action multiplier and its exact expiry time is not specified. Check the current event before relying on it.

The September 12 statement warns that cash exploits can cause timed bans and cash/XP resets. Promoting economy exploits or using XP-farming servers can lead to permanent bans. The advice here concerns legitimate team play, not exploit farming.

**Source status: Confirmed.**

## How the WARDOGS cash system works

Players start with $10,000. Before each life, cash can be used to assemble a custom loadout, and the remaining balance persists between matches.

This persistent economy connects individual spawn choices to later battles, rather than resetting every purchase decision at the end of a match.

**Source status: Confirmed.**

## Confirmed ways to earn cash

- Revive teammates.

- Transport friendly players.

- Help control the objective.

- Fight in the Hot Zone for the officially described double-cash opportunity.

## Beta-observed ways players earn cash

Community videos add a more detailed picture of the beta economy. Players talk about earning through healing, reviving, transport, logistics deliveries, repairing vehicles, building or destroying FOB equipment, spotting, assists, kills, vehicle kills and objective presence. These are useful guide topics, but exact payouts should be checked in the live build before publishing a farming route.

## Common spending pressure

The cash loop is also a spending loop. Community footage repeatedly shows players buying weapons, ammo, armor, helmets, backpacks, grenades, medical gear, specialist equipment, vehicle access, helicopter countermeasures, fuel and unlocks. The practical question for a cash guide is whether a life has earned back the equipment it consumed.

- Low-cost loadouts reduce risk but may limit your role.

- High-cost kits can pay off only if you live long enough to earn through the role.

- Vehicles can be team-critical, but fuel, ammo, flares and repair needs can turn them into money sinks.

- Revive bribes and tips create player-to-player incentives around support work.

## Beta money methods to test carefully

Transcript material from beta guides points to helicopter transport, spawn-vehicle deployment, long-range spotting or sniping, and vehicle play as high-interest money routes. These belong in a 'methods to test' section, not a guaranteed best-money guide, because balance can change before and during Early Access.

## A conservative preparation framework

Until item prices and balance are testable in the Early Access build, there is no evidence-backed best spending order. The useful pre-release principle is to connect each purchase to a clear role and keep in mind that unused cash carries forward.

- Choose the task first, then buy equipment for that task.

- Separate essential role equipment from optional upgrades.

- Review whether a vehicle purchase helps the current objective and team movement.
